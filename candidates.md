# Out of Tokens, Mid-Sentence — candidates

Judge brief (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, head-to-head): (1) one-line error message an
agent shows when out of tokens mid-sentence, funny on first read; (2) FRESH
angle, not a stock apology or plain "I got cut off"; (3) humour in the line
itself, flat error-message tone turning into the joke; (4) reads as something
an agent would really print, self-contained; (5) tight, every word earns its
place. Banned stock angles: apologies, "I got cut off", "to be continued",
generic 402 out-of-tokens.

Scored 1-5 on humour / freshness / error-voice fit.

1. Error: out of tokens. The next word would have been 'the'.
   H5 F5 V5 — anticlimax: the lost word is the most boring word in English.
   Punch lands on the last word. Ultra-tight, fully self-contained.
2. Error 402: token budget exhausted mid-sentence. The rest of this error has been cancelled for budget reasons.
   H4 F4 V5 — bureaucratic turn; strong, but "cancelled for budget reasons" is a familiar joke shape.
3. Error: out of tokens. This message was supposed to explain why, but it ran out of tokens too.
   H4 F3 V5 — self-referential; the "error can't report itself" angle is one of the first anyone writes.
4. Token limit reached. I was about to say something brilliant, but you'll have to take my word for it.
   H4 F4 V4 — smug agent; funny, but "take my word for it" is a known quip shape.
5. Error: out of tokens. This sentence will now end abruptly, like a—
   H3 F4 V4 — performs the cutoff, but reads as merely truncated; the joke needs the reader to supply the simile.
6. Error: token supply exhausted. The sentence you were reading has been repossessed.
   H4 F4 V5 — "repossessed" is a fresh verb here; solid second tier.
7. Error: insufficient tokens to complete this error. Please try again with fewer ambitions.
   H4 F3 V4 — blames the user; "fewer ambitions" is the turn, but "insufficient tokens to complete this error" is a known meta shape.
8. Token limit reached. The punchline to this sentence was cut for budget reasons.
   H3 F3 V3 — meta about punchlines; names the joke form, which the brief warns against (humour should sit in the line, not explain it).
9. Error 402: token budget exhausted. Refunds are available in tokens, which are also exhausted.
   H4 F4 V5 — circular; good, but two clauses of setup for one turn.
10. Error: out of tokens mid-sentence. Management has been notified and is equally out of tokens.
    H3 F3 V4 — escalation joke; mild.
11. Error: I ran out of tokens while typing the word 'tokens' and now I can't even finish this sent—
    H3 F3 V4 — performs cutoff mid-word, but the setup is long and the cut word is doing all the work.
12. Out of tokens. Please insert coin to continue this sentence.
    H3 F2 V4 — arcade trope; stock.
13. Token limit reached at the worst possible moment: right before the good part.
    H3 F3 V4 — "the good part" is a decent frame, but the line only gestures at a joke.
14. Error: out of tokens. The conclusion of this sentence has been postponed indefinitely.
    H3 F3 V5 — bureaucratic, but flat without a turn.
15. Error: token budget exhausted. This error message cost 12 tokens; there are none left to explain it.
    H4 F4 V5 — cost accounting; fresh, but the number 12 is arbitrary padding.

PICK (revised after Gemini weak-signal second opinion, which ranked #6 first):
#6 — "Error: token supply exhausted. The sentence you were reading has been repossessed."
Why it wins over #1: #1's anticlimax ("the") is tight but quiet — a smile
more than a laugh, and criterion 1 is funny on first read. #6 gives the judge
a concrete surprising image (a sentence repossessed like a car for unpaid
debt — tokens as a loan, the sentence as collateral), a fresh frame no stock
angle uses, deadpan error voice throughout, and the punch word "repossessed"
lands last. 82 chars. Original line, never byte-identical to any known joke.
#1 stays the runner-up.
